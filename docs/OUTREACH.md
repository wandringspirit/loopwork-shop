# Outreach: how to find buyers and close them

The plan for selling done-for-you shops to handmade sellers. Everything here is sent by you, by hand, one person at a time. Nothing is ever sent automatically.

**What you send people**
- India: https://loopwork.leadspilot.co/for-sellers/ or the PDF `docs/for-sellers.pdf`
- Outside India: https://loopwork.leadspilot.co/for-sellers/international/ or `docs/for-sellers-international.pdf`
- The demo shop itself: https://loopwork.leadspilot.co

---

## 1. Who to target first

The best prospect is a handmade seller who **already sells, but through DMs**:

- 500 to 20,000 Instagram followers, posting at least weekly.
- "DM to order", "DM for price" or a WhatsApp link in the bio, and no shop website (or just a Linktree).
- Comments full of "price?" and "is this available?".
- Products photographed reasonably well. You'll reuse their photos, so good photos make a shop that sells itself.

Good niches: crochet and knitting, candles, resin art, jewellery, embroidery, clay and pottery, home bakers, art prints, festive items (rakhi, Diwali hampers, Christmas ornaments).

**Outside India**, look for the same thing among sellers in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and Europe: Etsy sellers with an active Instagram. They pay Etsy roughly 10–15% of every sale, so "your own shop for your regulars" is easy to understand. They pay more, so they're worth more of your time.

## 2. Where to find them

| Place | How |
|---|---|
| **Instagram** (main channel) | Search hashtags such as #crochetindia, #handmadeinindia, #smallbusinessindia, #crochetersofinstagram, #handmadeuk, #etsyseller, #makersgonnamake. Open recent posts, not "top", so you find active small sellers. |
| **Etsy** (to find, not to contact) | Search a product (e.g. "crochet bunny"), open shops with sales and an Instagram link, and contact them **on Instagram**. Don't pitch through Etsy messages: Etsy's rules forbid unsolicited promotion there and can close your account. |
| **Facebook groups** | Handmade sellers' and small business groups. Read each group's rules; many allow a weekly promotion thread. Be helpful first. |
| **Craft fairs and markets** (India) | Show the demo shop on your phone and hand over the PDF or a QR code to `/for-sellers/`. In person, people say yes fastest. |
| **People you know** | Ask friends and family who they know who sells something handmade. A warm introduction beats 50 cold DMs. |

## 3. The offer that gets the first three clients

You have no clients yet, so make saying yes easy:

1. **Founding price:** the first 3 clients get the Shop plan at the Starter price (₹4,999 / $299), in return for a short testimonial and permission to show their shop on the sales page.
2. **Free preview:** offer to make a private preview with 3 or 4 of their products before they pay anything. Ask first ("Can I use 3 of your photos to make you a free preview?"). Then run `npm run starter`, add their name and those products, deploy it to a private preview address (`*.workers.dev`), and send the link. Seeing their own products in a real shop is what sells it.
3. **Half up front, half at launch.** Low risk for them, safe for you.

When you have 3 happy clients, drop the founding price and add their shops and quotes to the sales pages.

## 4. Daily routine

- **20 personalised DMs a day.** Fewer, better messages beat volume. Instagram restricts accounts that paste the same message dozens of times, especially new accounts.
- Use a business Instagram account for this, with the demo shop or `/for-sellers/` in the bio. Post the demo shop and, later, client shops.
- **Track everyone** in a Google Sheet: date, name, handle, country, niche, followers, message sent, reply, preview sent, status (no reply / interested / preview / paid / lost), follow-up date.
- **One follow-up only**, 4–5 days later, and only if they haven't replied. If someone says no, thank them and stop.

Rough numbers to expect (varies a lot): 100 DMs → 10–15 replies → 4–5 previews → 1–2 sales. Personalised messages and free previews push this up.

## 6. Answers to common objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "I sell fine on Instagram." | "Keep Instagram, it's where people find you. The shop is where they buy: prices, sizes and what's in stock are all in one place, and orders come in complete instead of over 20 messages." |
| "It's too expensive." | India: "Shopify alone is about ₹1,500 a month, ₹18,000 a year before apps. This is a one-time [price], and hosting is free." Abroad: "Shopify is about $29–39 a month, every month. This is one payment, and Etsy takes 10–15% of every sale." |
| "I'm not technical." | "You don't need to be. You send photos and prices, and I do everything. With the care plan you just WhatsApp me changes." |
| "What if I need changes later?" | "The care plan covers it: [₹499 / $29] a month, cancel any time. Or pay per change." |
| "Etsy brings me buyers." | "Keep Etsy for new buyers. Send your Instagram followers and repeat customers to your own shop, where you keep the whole sale." |
| "Can customers pay online?" | "Yes, that's an add-on: Razorpay (UPI, cards) in India, or Stripe and PayPal abroad." |
| "Is it only for crochet?" | "No, the demo is crochet, but it works for anything handmade: candles, jewellery, bakes, prints." |

## 7. Getting paid

**India:** UPI or bank transfer, 50% to start and 50% before launch. Send a simple invoice (name, plan, amount, date). Below the GST threshold you don't charge GST; see the `india-solopreneur-tax-accounting` notes or ask a CA once income grows.

**Outside India: PayPal invoice in USD**, 50% to start and 50% before launch. The client can pay with a PayPal account or a card.
- Your PayPal India account must be fully verified before you can receive money (PAN, bank micro-deposits, ID, purpose code). If it stays "in review" for more than a week, escalate through the Message Center (the `paypal-india-merchant-operator` notes have the exact steps).
- Set the purpose code to software or website services (for example `P0802`, software consulting and implementation).
- PayPal India can't hold dollars: payments are converted to rupees and sent to your bank automatically. Keep PayPal's FIRA/FIRC records. They prove export income, which matters for GST (exports of services can be zero-rated under an LUT if you register) and income tax.
- PayPal's fees and currency conversion take a few percent; the international prices already allow for that.
- Until PayPal is verified, you can still take your first international client with Wise or a bank SWIFT transfer, or Razorpay international cards.

## 8. After they say yes

Follow "A client's shop" in [DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md): `npm run starter`, set it up in the admin, make their logo, run the launch check and deploy to **their** Cloudflare account and domain. Then:
- Test an order with them on a call.
- Ask for the testimonial and a screenshot of the shop for the sales page.
- Offer the care plan.
- Ask: "Do you know another seller who'd like one?" Offer them a discount for each referral that buys.

## Rules

1. One person at a time, always personalised. No bulk messages, bots or bought lists.
2. One follow-up at most. "No" means stop.
3. Never use a client's photos publicly without asking.
4. Only promise what exists or what you'll build before their launch (see [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md)).
5. People in the UK and EU have stronger privacy rights, even sole traders. Keep messages one-to-one, don't add them to mailing lists, and delete their details if they ask.
